Building an AI product from idea to prototype: the PiLENS story

How we approached PiLENS, our first product: the problem, the limits we set ourselves, how the system works and the numbers we measured in our tests.

PiVisen AI is a technology start-up from Islamabad, Pakistan. We build AI products and software for businesses. Our first product is PiLENS, an AI security camera unit. This article explains how we approached it: the problem we chose, the limits we set ourselves, how the system works and what we measured.

The problem

Cameras are everywhere: in shops, pharmacies, offices and homes. Most of them only record. Nobody has time to watch the screens all day, so the footage is usually looked at after something has gone wrong, when it is too late to act.

AI can watch those cameras and raise an alert while something is happening. But many AI security systems are built for large organisations. They expect powerful computers with graphics cards (GPUs), or they send video to the cloud for analysis. For a small business or a family, that can mean high costs, a dependence on the internet and private video leaving the building.

We wanted to see whether we could do better for those people.

The limits we set ourselves

PiLENS was designed around five rules. They shaped every technical decision.

  • Small and low-cost. PiLENS had to run on a Raspberry Pi 5, a small and affordable computer.
  • No GPU. All the AI had to run on the device's ordinary processor (CPU).
  • Video stays on the premises. Footage is processed on the device, inside the building.
  • Real time. The system has to keep up with live video, not catch up later.
  • Alerts people can trust. The owner should only be notified about events that have been checked.

The first three rules made the engineering harder. The last two decided whether anyone would actually want to use it.

How PiLENS works

PiLENS follows three simple steps.

  1. Watch. The camera feeds live video to the PiLENS unit on site. PiLENS looks at the video in short clips, each 2.4 seconds long, because actions like theft or a fight only make sense as movement over time.
  2. Detect and double-check. The AI on the device checks each clip for signs of theft or violent incidents. When it finds one, a second AI check reviews the event before going further.
  3. Alert. Only events confirmed by the second check are sent to the owner.

The second check is the part we care about most. A detector that looks at every clip has to be quick and alert, which means it will sometimes react to harmless things. Instead of making that first stage less sensitive, and risking missed incidents, PiLENS adds a second AI check that confirms each flagged event before the owner is notified.

What we measured

We tested PiLENS against the limits we had set. These are the results from our tests:

  • Checks each 2.4-second video clip in about 0.14 seconds on a Raspberry Pi 5 CPU.
  • Ran 2 hours live at 30 frames per second without dropping a frame.
  • A second AI check cut false alerts on normal test videos from 16.4% to 3.6%.

In plain terms: each clip is checked far faster than it takes to happen, so the system keeps up with live video with time to spare. It stayed stable during a continuous live run. And the second check removed most of the false alerts on ordinary footage where nothing bad was happening, which is exactly the kind of footage a real shop produces most of the day.

What we took away

A few lessons from building PiLENS now guide how we work:

  • Write the limits down first. Clear rules such as "no GPU" and "video stays on site" made hard choices much easier.
  • Treat false alarms as a product problem. They decide whether people keep the alerts switched on. We explain why in Why false alarms matter in AI security.
  • Measure normal days, not only incidents. Testing on ordinary footage tells you what living with a system will feel like.
  • Small hardware is enough for more than people expect. With careful design, useful real-time AI can run on a low-cost device.

What's next

PiLENS is our first product, and we are continuing to develop it for the places it was designed for: shops, pharmacies, offices and homes. You can read more on the PiLENS product page.

If you would like PiLENS at your premises, or you have your own idea for an AI product, we would be glad to hear from you.
