Why false alarms matter in AI security, and how to reduce them

False alarms teach people to ignore alerts. Here is why AI security systems raise them, and seven practical ways to cut them down.

25 October 2026 4 min read By PiVisen AI Team

  2. Why AI systems raise false alarms

  4. Seven ways to reduce false alarms
  5. What to ask a supplier

Share this article

Most conversations about AI security focus on one question: does it catch the bad things? That matters. But there is a second question that decides whether a system is actually useful in daily life: how often does it cry wolf?

The real cost of a false alarm

Each false alarm costs a little time. Someone stops what they are doing, opens the app, looks at the clip and decides it was nothing. On its own, that is a minor annoyance.

The bigger cost is what false alarms do to people over time. After a few days of alerts about shadows, cleaners and customers bending down to tie a shoelace, the owner starts to glance at notifications less carefully. Then they mute them. Then the one alert that matters arrives, and nobody looks.

This is called alert fatigue, and it is the quiet way that many security systems fail. A system that catches everything but is ignored protects nothing. That is why we treat false alarms as a core product problem, not a detail to tidy up later.

Why AI systems raise false alarms

AI models learn what an event looks like from examples. When they see something that looks a bit like those examples, they react. Common triggers include:

  • Light changes: headlights sweeping across a window, a door opening onto bright sunshine, lights switching on.
  • Shadows and reflections on glass, polished floors or display cases.
  • Harmless but unusual behaviour: children running, staff restocking quickly, someone carrying a large box.
  • Difficult camera angles where shelves or people block part of the view.
  • New situations the model never saw while learning, like a different uniform or a seasonal display.

The trade-off you cannot avoid

Every detection system has a sensitivity setting. Turn it up and you catch more real events, but you also get more false alarms. Turn it down and the false alarms drop, but you start missing things. There is no setting that makes both problems disappear, whatever a sales brochure says.

The goal is to change the system itself so that, at a useful sensitivity, false alarms are rare. Here is how.

Seven ways to reduce false alarms

1. Fix the camera before the AI

Many false alarms start with a poor view. Reposition cameras away from direct light, raise them above obstructions and add lighting where the scene is dark. Small changes here often help more than any software setting.

2. Use zones and schedules

Not every part of the picture matters all the time. Ignore the busy pavement outside the window. Only watch the stockroom door after closing. Simple rules like these remove whole categories of false alarms.

3. Look at movement over time

Systems that judge single frames are easily fooled by a strange pose or a reflection. Looking at short clips lets the AI see what actually happened, which is much harder to confuse.

4. Add a second check before alerting

This is one of the most effective steps. When the first stage flags an event, a second AI check reviews the same moment before anyone is notified. The first stage can stay alert and quick, and the second stage filters out the mistakes.

From our PiLENS tests: A second AI check cut false alerts on normal test videos from 16.4% to 3.6%.

5. Tune for the site

Every shop, office and home is different. Testing the system on footage from the place where it will be installed shows which situations confuse it there, so settings can be adjusted before it goes live.

6. Learn from feedback

Give users a simple way to mark an alert as wrong. Those examples are valuable: they show exactly where the system needs to improve and can be used to make the next version better.

7. Measure honestly

You cannot improve what you do not measure. Count false alarms on ordinary footage where nothing bad happens, not only on clips of incidents. That number tells you what living with the system will feel like.

What to ask a supplier

  • How do you measure false alarms, and on what kind of footage?
  • What happens between detection and the alert? Is there a second check?
  • Can I set zones and schedules myself?
  • How do I report a wrong alert, and what happens to that report?

Be careful with any promise that a system never raises a false alarm. Every AI system makes some mistakes. The honest goal is a system you can trust enough to keep the notifications switched on. That is the standard we hold PiLENS to.
