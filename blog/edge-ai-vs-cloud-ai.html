Edge AI vs cloud AI: when to use which

Should your AI run on a small device on site or on servers in the cloud? A practical comparison of speed, cost, privacy and upkeep, with a simple guide.

When a business decides to use AI, the first technical question is often not "which model?" but "where should it run?". The answer affects how fast the system reacts, what it costs every month, who can see your data and what happens when the internet goes down.

There are two basic options, edge AI and cloud AI, and a third that combines them. Here is how to choose without getting lost in jargon.

What edge AI means

"Edge" simply means close to where the data is created. With edge AI, the model runs on a small computer installed on site: next to a camera, on a factory line or inside a shop. The video or sensor data is processed right there, and usually only the result leaves the building, such as "a person entered the stockroom at 22:14".

Edge devices today range from single-board computers like the Raspberry Pi to small boxes with dedicated AI chips. They are far less powerful than a data centre, so models have to be designed and tuned carefully to run on them.

What cloud AI means

With cloud AI, the data is sent over the internet to servers run by a cloud provider. The model runs there, and the result is sent back or shown on a dashboard. Cloud servers can be very powerful and you can rent more of them when you need them, which makes the cloud the natural home for heavy work such as training models or analysing months of records.

Five questions that decide it

1. How fast do you need an answer?

If the system has to react within a second or two, for example to alert someone about an incident, every trip across the internet adds delay and risk. Edge AI answers locally. Cloud AI is fine when a few seconds or minutes do not matter, such as a daily report.

2. How reliable is your internet connection?

A cloud system that loses its connection stops thinking. An edge system keeps working and can send its results later. For sites with patchy connections, this alone can settle the question.

3. How sensitive is the data?

Video of customers, staff or family members is personal. Keeping it on site reduces the number of places it can leak from and makes privacy easier to explain. Some organisations also have rules about where data may be stored.

4. What will it cost to run?

Video is heavy. Streaming several cameras to the cloud all day uses a lot of bandwidth, and cloud processing is usually billed for as long as it runs. Edge AI costs more at the start, because you buy a device, but it can be much cheaper over months and years. For light data such as text or occasional photos, the cloud is often cheaper and simpler.

5. How many sites, and how often will the model change?

Updating one model in the cloud is easy. Updating fifty devices in fifty locations needs proper tooling. If you have many sites or expect frequent changes, plan for remote updates from day one, or lean towards the cloud.

The hybrid option

Many real systems use both. The edge device does the urgent, private work: watching video and raising alerts. The cloud does the rest: storing small summaries, showing dashboards across many sites and training better models. Only small, useful pieces of information travel over the internet, not raw footage.

Some everyday examples

  • Security alerts in a shop or pharmacy: edge. Speed and privacy matter most, and video is heavy.
  • Checking products on a production line: usually edge. The line cannot wait for the internet.
  • Searching a month of recordings for patterns: cloud. It is a heavy job that does not need instant results.
  • An assistant that answers questions about your documents: usually cloud, because the models involved need a lot of computing power.
  • A chain of branches with a head office dashboard: hybrid.

How we approach it

At PiVisen AI we lean towards the edge whenever cameras and privacy are involved, because that is where the cost and trust benefits are biggest. Our own product, PiLENS, runs entirely on a Raspberry Pi 5 without a GPU and keeps video on the premises. But we use the cloud whenever a job needs it. The right answer depends on your site, not on a trend.

If you are weighing up the options for a project, a short conversation is usually enough to narrow it down.
